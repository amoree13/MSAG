About

I'm a BS Nursing student at Ateneo de Davao University, School of Nursing. Outside of clinical coursework, I put together design and communication projects — event materials, a personal brand, and infographics that try to make a small problem easier to see and act on.

My personal brand, de Amor closet, is a small play on my own name — Amor is Spanish for love, and the closet is built around giving pre-loved things a second story rather than treating them as used up. The same idea carries into how I approach nursing: paying attention to what's already there before adding something new.

My tagline, Nursing – Compassion – Story – Craft, is the short version of how I try to work: caring about people first, listening for the story behind a problem, and being careful about the craft of whatever I make — whether that's a care plan or a poster.

Selected work

Class projects & design work

Event Poster · Activity 1

Free Coffee — Arrupe Hall, Booth 7

A poster for a free-coffee giveaway booth at the School of Nursing, meant to be readable from across a hallway and to feel like an invitation rather than an announcement.

Why I made this The brief was simple: get people to stop at the booth. I leaned on a big steaming coffee cup and playful, bubbly lettering so it would read as friendly at a glance — "no catch, just caffeine and good vibes" is the whole pitch, and the design needed to match that tone instead of looking like an ad.

Personal Brand · Activity 2

de Amor closet

A brand identity for a pre-loved closet concept: a line-drawn cat mark, a script-and-serif wordmark, and the tagline "Where Every Item Has a Story."

Why I made this The name is a small pun on mine — de Amor, "of love" — and I wanted the identity to feel that warm and personal rather than like a generic shop. The cat mark and the handwritten-feeling italic in the wordmark are there so the brand reads as lived-in, matching a closet built on things that already have a history.

Infographic · Activity 3

Coping with Financial Blind Spots in Student Spending

An infographic on why student spending is hard to track — split across cash, e-wallets, and bank transfers — with the data behind it and a step-by-step tracking solution: record, categorize, set limits, monitor, forecast, get alerted, and improve.

Why I made this Small purchases scattered across three different payment methods are exactly the kind of thing that quietly leads to overspending, and I wanted to make that blind spot visible with real numbers rather than just saying "budget better." The seven-step flow at the bottom is meant to be something a student could actually follow, not just a diagram.
